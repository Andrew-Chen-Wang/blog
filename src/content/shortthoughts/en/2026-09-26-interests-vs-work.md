People develop interests because they get good at it. Sometimes, it's useful for society like mathematics, but it's paid horribly. Sometimes a teen prodigy stumbles on Python and makes a billion-dollar startup. Sometimes the developed skills are completely societally useless like Scrabble competitors learning so many words (it's a hard game, there is strategy which can help educate people on logical thinking, strategy, etc. I know, I know.).

Whenever someone says "I wish I could just do my hobby instead of work", "capitalism enslaves people in jobs they don't like", and "we should let people do what they want", it completely misses the point that money is just a means of survival from an individual's perspective. Humans survive because they contribute to society or historically their tribe. Most peoples' interests don't produce that much money except for a select few for marketing purposes because the economic value for tons of interest don't matter.

There's an interesting game that anti-abortion groups play: if a baby isn't born, what if we missed the next Einstein? Besides purely ignoring the requirement that society is set up in a way that an Einstein could achieve greatness (i.e. the German's university system was amazing), someone would need the interest in physics to begin with. The requirement that society could lose out on an Einstein is selfish of a generation because it asks that society ensure the nurturing of a genius to help others. Interests require self-stumbling into an introduction and natural keenness. Einstein is a genius because of his natural keenness with no expectation to become a genius. Instead of banning abortions, providing children with more opportunities to discover interests would be better.

In Soviet Russia and China, people were nurtured into programs like engineering because of communism (capitalism was required to create the abundance needed for socialist utopia, and engineering was the way to achieve fast growth in wealth), and I think that's fine. Tons of CS students probably have no interest in CS, but they developed the skills to make money which contributes to society in that they are fixing problems.

Lots of interests don't pay well if at all, and maybe Einstein would be broke today. His contributions in general relativity only became useful in the practical world very late in his life and probably after his death i.e. space travel. People go into environmental studies because they care about the environment, then they realize they don't get paid much, so they enter the oil industry and get ignored by executives. But interests don't have to be useful; they become useful when some economic value or inspiration can be derived (e.g. Steve Jobs explaining calligraphy helped shaped better fonts for Apple, or the bird that inspired the Japanese bullet train design i.e. biomimicry), but interests don't have to make money. It's a derivation of joy that sometimes happens to make money. Let interests be interests and let people enjoy them and advance the fields of those interests. Maybe they'll be useful one day.

The point of work is to continually contribute to society; work feels meaningless to many because it feels so disconnected from communal survival, only individual survival, but we have to remember that specialization made us feel disconnected after thousands of years of close-knit tribal communities. The work people do today is helping someone, even if it's extremely indirect, because work is all about solving problems, and workers can survive because they helped their tribe of 8 billion people live another day.

## AI

Thankfully, modern democratic socialists have come around to requiring people to work in basically an economic system that is akin to modern liberal economics (plus of course a humongous corporate and income tax at whatever level bureaucrats determine). However, AI's advancements are alarming. I believe AI trumps average human intelligence and obviously human knowledge. Let's imagine a future where AI physically and intelligently outpaces humanity and self improves, rendering people unneeded for advancing society.

If it's solely up to the robots, do we forfeit our future to their rules? Will they handicap their own growth to limit energy expenditure? Will they start a human population k-curve growth to not necessitate advancement in technology? Who sets the rules of whoever set up the robots and society's end state? Should we not be controlling the robots and continuously, as culture and advancements change, continue reforming government of human society? Who will step up to govern society if everyone else is laid back doing nothing, if not the robots? To me, it's all or nothing: either we give robots our future society, or we, as a society, culturally decide we all must still contribute somehow in helping each other with robots simply assisting. I don't think there can be an in-between where a portion of the population decides not to work while the other doesn't. Someone needs to be around to do human only work like governing and general administrative oversight of said hypothetical robots.
