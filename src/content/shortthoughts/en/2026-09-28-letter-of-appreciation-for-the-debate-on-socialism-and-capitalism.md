I'm kind of getting back into writing recently as I've had a lot of time to think about ideas I've had simmering for at least 2 years, though I do think the writing could be a bit smoother.

I wanted to clarify that I don't really believe in socialism. I think my dad has convinced me enough that socialism as a societal organizational methodology falls prey to greed for power via human nature itself, fails at bureaucracy again from human nature, and ultimately will have multiple issues from a system designed for a culture of group cohesion (and as my Cuban Communist friend agrees with me in that a socialist society at scale must become a religion) is doomed to fail at scale because human nature dictates the need for selfishness. Instead, what I found more interesting was the leverage of power. My college roommate was a dual economics and sociology major, and he said he had to hide his identity from both sides, and I can now understand why. A lot of economics is focused on the former free market theorem of healthy self regulating markets, but that doesn't seem like that's the case since we've already had to have anti-trust laws. Sociology is interesting because, to me, it has to do with both power and the individual human condition. Unfortunately, it's practically hacked by progressive hive mind group think to the point of political inflexibility which has led to both sociologists and someone like Brett Kavanaugh (who was liked by both liberals and conservatives prior to SCOTUS) to turn extreme and ignorant. To elaborate a tad on that point, sociology majors tend to dislike economics, so it seems any speak of an inch of power from one group over another creates a unanimous need to squash it when to me it's simply human nature. [Here's citation on sociology dying from academic inflexibility due to personal political activism squashing dissenting opinions by claiming "pure objectivism" and bending to social media groupthink and thus continued growth towards extremism](https://www.wsj.com/us-news/education/is-it-time-to-liquidate-the-study-of-sociology-6b85ac14). I also discussed this article on Reddit, and people simply rejected my anecdote of being completely dismissed of any idea rather than debate me wholeheartedly. Firstly, the sociology group first refused to debate lively with me showed the political nature of the time. Secondly, on an online forum of sociology enthused folks, my anecdote was ironically dismissed with disbelief and accusations by those who say sociology is designed to be open for debate, protecting their discipline over addressing the potential that stigma and dismissal is alive in their community hence the article. The article itself says the necessity of sociology which I agree with, and they are great at discovering the problems, but the political activism ends up resolving with extreme solutions and thus discrediting of said group.

I do appreciate the requirement to save the end customer from socialist ideology. Though I don't agree with basically every socialist (including modern socialists who have incorporated modern microeconomics as a sign of acknowledging human nature, bureaucracy, and small or individual, private mediation being more efficient) that we should outright tax companies and individuals for simply being rich, I do agree that the power of money should be regulated in the benefit of the customer while balancing the health of the business offering products and services from the distinct, narrow perspective of ensuring healthy market competition. Socialists tend to forget that in order to have innovation and technology, over-regulation and monitoring increases bureaucratic burden on both the government and business, and creates fear to even start a business. On the contrary, capitalists who are scared of Democrats over-regulating and over-taxing to scare off all business opportunities forget that large enough financial and personal incentives will always attract founders to start businesses. I want to conclude by appreciating the balancing nature, and I believe modern economics is slowly balancing practicality of society with olden theories. The necessity to view potential theories as being useful in different cases, for how long, and what context is becoming the new norm, and I believe we'll have more intellectual conversations between opposing factions.
