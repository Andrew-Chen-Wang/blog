I'm not the smartest, i want to help people, and i want to be right in how i go about doing so. right in what i think will help people and my country the most.






Some people say they want to become president. Some say they want to be a billionaire. Some say they wanted to just bask in nature's glory. Some wanted to become environmentalists to become advocates in our age of climate change. People would ask "what are you going to do in life?" I didn't grow up having life goals. I didn't necessarily have passions either. I could start learning anything and convince myself some topic is interesting and be in a 2-week rabbit hole. Though I grew up talking about politics and business a lot with my family, I hadn't necessarily developed a passion for anything.

As I continued aging, I just thought what was I lacking? During college decisions, I felt like I lacked a career direction. I settled on the natural next step for young adults: I needed to decide a money-making career to be self-sustaining and be comfortable. I started learning how to code during my senior year of high school, coding 16 hours a day during the pandemic. I loved the dopamine rush from building a product and pride in debugging complex situations. For college, I chose to pursue a business degree to learn how to run a successful, growing business instead of being a wage slave. Though career planning seems like long term thinking, for me, it was quite short-term. The purpose of charting which career I'd go down was money. Money would allow me to have a low-stress life, to raise a family, to be financially comfortable, and to do hobbies that I wanted.

So down I went: I tried building a startup. I tried getting into good programming jobs that would help me excel professionally quicker and thus get more money faster.





Really, I did have some kind of passion, but it didn't lie in an interest, passion, or topic. I just liked helping people. During the pandemic, my dad started an assisted living center. It has been unprofitable for 4 years, but the family supports it because it's his first long-running business operation, just subsidized by his salary.




I think for many people, the answer would be yes. For me, I felt like I knew who I was as a person, I had particular interests that I liked thinking about more than other topics.

In 2022, I figured I wasn't really excelling or being the best or being important enough for anything. I had become a walking fun fact generator, open source maintainer, go-to Python person for places I worked at, and started learning about startups as my life career. It was fun.

Because my dad and my sibling talked about business and politics (they did neither) during dinner every evening, I had developed a somewhat more advanced interest in those topics. But the underlying reason was I liked helping people (who doesn't? Chemically, it's very rewarding).

- Setting goals that were personal
- Not accomplishing them
- Pivoting and focusing on what I want to do
- Finding purpose and doing life goals (2026-05-07)
- Resetting my annual goals around my life goals while still having the fun in them


Since 2022, I've laid out a list of dozens of goals including (re-)learning languages, athletic goals, career goals, hobby goals, and life goals. By the end of each year, I manage to accomplish two goals. I wasn't always a goal setter, and every year my goals changed.

Growing up, I didn't really have a life goal besides helping people. I didn't really know how to go about it, but I narrowed down what my family always talked about during dinner: politics (more on the human nature and ideological side but practical), what it means to be altruistic in society vs. being selfish to advance ourselves and our family, and making money. My family is full of big dreamers, but we were all making small businesses, and only doing well sometimes.

I attended University of Michigan's business school after declining a STEM major in biology, economics, statistics, or computer science (which I ended up dual majoring in) in other colleges I'd been accepted to. At the time, my family was all concentrated in Michigan, UMich has a good public and business school, and I didn't really know what I wanted to make at the time.

In 2022:
- I wanted to help my grandma see doctors and acclimate her to new technology as I'd moved to NYC
- Work on my first AI/LLM startup (it was customer service)
- Adjust to my new adult life post-dropping out of college. Make new friends, actually do more adult responsibilities and financial planning, and indulge in my urbanist (I'm not a purist; I'm practical and realistic about urbanist expectations for any country)

Of those goals, my grandma can FaceTime or call me regularly, re-lives educational trauma (she studies the week before my sibling arrives home, and my sibling then tests her), and . I dropped out

In 2023, in addition to a year-end list, I set about a Goals by Decade list.
