Transcript from my group chat:

Yea I followed the race closely and am really scared about the Democratic Party because of primary voters. The problem with primary voters in the democratic base is that they are hyper-charged and woefully do not represent the vast majority of the Democratic base. Noah Smith talks about a bit https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/what-if-our-biggest-problems-arent

The argument is that the socialist DSA movement and Gen Z and millennials are actually in decent economic shape compared to income/cost basis of Baby Boomers i.e. baby boomers had it the same. It also doesn’t help that the DSA is strangely overly wealthy, with a high percentage being 6 figure college graduates. It’s probably why the Gen Z and the DSA’s primary issues stem from non-economic standpoints like abolishing the US senate, Israel/Palestine, etc. and so the race in Michigan senate primary was primarily focused on social issues rather than economic which the core voter base care about.

I think Yang’s point about independents in red states is correct, and the toxicity associated with the party is why I initially supported Mike Duggan for Governor. In battleground states, even an independent should caucus, though. In red states, I can see it’s fine to run independent? Depends on how big of a red state it is; if it’s tiny, then sure. This is why Mike Duggan dropped out: he had the merit of revitalizing Detroit as mayor, but as a large state, he needed the Democratic party's backing, but, because he didn't caucus with them (poor political strategy), and he was acquiring backers that were in a high income bracket, started leaning MAGA (he's a lifelong Democrat) to attract swing voters (which never works in the large scheme of voting).

Yang discussed UBI again, and wealth distribution. I’m actually increasingly more confident that trillion dollar companies will gobble up markets, and I detail it in my blog here: https://andrewcwang.com/short-thoughts/2026-08-19-ai-data-centers-and-monopolization/

“He especially likes activities where a child is working toward a concrete goal with other humans” This is a very male-oriented response, though I guess it’s important given men are dropping out of the workforce substantially compared to women. It's also vital for everyone, but I think Yang in particular as a male would like collaborative environments.

"Don't ship slop" this is a management problem. It either means slop was created before, and now there can be blamed that’s placed, but in reality, it should be forcing companies to make certain elements of their business higher quality such as their end products or certain positive externalities (like a slide show can be designed better story-wise, not just decoratively).

I agree on “Yang and Zach therefore expect a countertrend toward the analog / physical / human” though, and it’s already seen pre-AI.
